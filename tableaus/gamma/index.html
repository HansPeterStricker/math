The tableau

Euler's constant γ = 0.5772156649… is the limit of 1 + 1/2 + ⋯ + 1/n − ln n. It is less famous than π and e, but it appears in as many different places: in sums and integrals, in the gamma and zeta functions, in probability and in the distribution of divisors and primes. Unlike π and e, it is not even known whether γ is irrational.

The formulas are arranged by form: sum, integral and limit. Formulas that are statements about the gamma, digamma or zeta function stand under special functions, formulas about divisors and primes under number theory. γ stands alone on the left. Formulas that really give eγ are written with the logarithm. Formulas that serve as a definition of γ are marked.

Derivations

Under “Proof idea” (click to open) each card names what it follows from: its premises, the kind of method and the method. Often a premise is a theorem about one of the functions in the tab “Functions”. All derivations start from a definition of γ. The buttons at the top right choose which one: harmonic numbers (γ = lim (Hn − ln n)), gamma function (γ = −Γ′(1)) or zeta function (γ is the constant term of ζ(s) at s = 1). The other two definitions are then theorems.

Every formula except Grönwall's lim sup has been checked numerically (tests/check-values.js).

The graph

Statements without premises are the chosen definition of γ and the functions whose foundations do not contain γ; they stand one column before the first statement that uses them. The columns are ordered by the length of the longest chain of derivations. An arrow leads from a premise to the statement that follows from it. Hovering over an arrow shows the whole proof: premises, kind of method and method. A click on a node opens its card. The graph can be moved by dragging and zoomed with the mouse wheel.
