    About this graph

    Each point is a theorem, lemma, corollary or conjecture of representation theory (MSC 20C, 22). An arrow from A to B means that a usual proof of B uses A. Where there are several proofs, one has been chosen; its idea is in the panel.

    Each MSC class has its own sector of the circle. The distance from the centre shows the level within the area: within a sector a theorem lies further out than the theorems its proof uses. The colour shows the MSC class; larger points are used, directly or indirectly, by more proofs.

    Results proved in another area are not points of this graph. When a theorem whose proof uses one of them is selected, an arrow comes from outside, from a small point with its name in the colour of that area; a click on the point opens the graph of that area with the theorem marked. The switch “arrows from other areas” shows all these arrows. Arrows to other areas lead from a theorem to the theorems of other areas whose proofs use it. The panel also links the concepts occurring in a statement to the concept graphs.

    Indirect dependencies (A → C when C also uses B and B depends on A) and edges used only by other proofs (dashed) are shown only with their switches on the left.

    A click on a theorem lays the card of its proof over the dimmed graph: the theorem at the top with the theorems its proof uses directly below it (brown; the arrows point up to the theorem that uses them). A click on any box of the card shows or hides the boxes below it; a theorem with several proofs has a small switch “1/3 ›” for the next one. A right click lays the card of the consequences: the theorem at the top, below it the theorems that use it in some proof (blue; dashed where not every proof uses it); a click on a box shows or hides the boxes below it. The card closes with ×, Escape or a click outside it and starts afresh the next time. The small “↗” at the lower left of a box opens the card of that node (for a node of another area its own graph); “‹ Back” returns to the previous card. With the switch “click opens a card” turned off, a click marks instead, in the graph itself, the theorems its proof uses directly, and each further click on it one step more; a right click marks in the same way the theorems that use it. The panel shows the statement, the proof idea and, in the same drawing, all theorems it builds on. Pointing at a theorem shows its name at once; after half a second only this theorem stays lit. Drag a theorem to move it, drag the background to move the picture, use the mouse wheel to zoom. The search field finds theorems by name.

    The contents were compiled with the help of a language model and have not been checked by a specialist.
