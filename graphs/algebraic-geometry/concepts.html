    About this graph

    Each point is a definition from algebraic geometry (MSC 14). An arrow from A to B means that the definition of B uses A. Dashed lines mean “every B is an A”.

    Each MSC class has its own sector of the circle. The distance from the centre shows the level within the area: concepts from other areas (prerequisites) are on the innermost circle, and within a sector a concept lies further out than the concepts its definition uses. The colour shows the MSC class; larger points are used, directly or indirectly, by more definitions.

    Concepts with a dashed border are defined in another area; their panel links to the graph of that area.

    “Is a” relations and edges from prerequisites such as ℕ and ℤ appear when a concept is selected; indirect dependencies (A → C when C also uses B and B depends on A) only with their switch on the left.

    Point at a concept to see the concepts its definition uses; click it to read the definition and to mark everything it depends on (brown). The switch “concepts using it” marks instead everything that depends on it (blue). The panel on the right shows the definitions it builds on. Drag a concept to move it, drag the background to move the picture, use the mouse wheel to zoom. The search field finds concepts by name; the concept found is ringed in yellow and the view is centred on it.

    The contents were compiled with the help of a language model and have not been checked by a specialist.
