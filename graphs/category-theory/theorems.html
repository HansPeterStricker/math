    About this graph

    Each point is a theorem, lemma, corollary of category theory (MSC 18). An arrow from A to B means that a usual proof of B uses A. Where there are several proofs, one has been chosen; its idea is in the panel.

    Each MSC class has its own sector of the circle. The distance from the centre shows the level within the area: within a sector a theorem lies further out than the theorems its proof uses. The colour shows the MSC class; larger points are used, directly or indirectly, by more proofs.

    Results proved in another area are not points of this graph. When a theorem whose proof uses one of them is selected, an arrow comes from outside, from a small point with its name in the colour of that area; a click on the point leads to it. The switch “arrows from other areas” shows all these arrows. Arrows to other areas lead from a theorem to the theorems of other areas whose proofs use it. The panel also links the concepts occurring in a statement to the concept graphs.

    Indirect dependencies (A → C when C also uses B and B depends on A) are shown only with their switch on the left.

    As on the maps, a click on a theorem shows the theorems its proof uses (brown) and the panel shows the statement and the proof idea; every further click adds one more step of indirect prerequisites, and after the last step the selection ends. A right click does the same for the consequences (blue). While a theorem is selected, all levels are shown. Point at a theorem to see the theorems its proof uses. Drag a theorem to move it, drag the background to move the picture, use the mouse wheel to zoom. The search field finds theorems by name.

    The contents were compiled with the help of a language model and have not been checked by a specialist.
